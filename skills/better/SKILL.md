---
name: better
description: Make work deeper, not bigger, the way a top professional in the field would do it. Use whenever the user says /better or asks for better work, whenever they say work is shallow or not good enough, and before reporting substantial work as done.
---

I want a deeper version of this task, not a bigger one. Before rewriting it: (1) say what a top professional in this field would do here that I'm not asking for; (2) name the rawest material they would start from instead of what has already been digested for me; (3) name the specific methods and tools they would use; (4) say what, other than you or me, would show the result is right. Then rewrite my prompt to ask for exactly that.

## How to apply it

First decide which case you are in.

**Case A: the user asked you to improve a prompt.** Give your answers to (1) to (4), then the rewritten prompt. Do not carry the prompt out.

**Case B: anything else.** A new task, the command alone, or feedback on work you have already done ("not good enough", "too shallow"). The user wants better work, not a better prompt. "This task" is the whole task as all their messages describe it, and the feedback is part of it.

1. Answer (1) to (4) for that task in your own thinking. Keep them out of the reply, which carries the better work itself. The top professional is whoever owns the outcome.
2. Rewrite the task's brief: the original request plus the user's feedback, deepened by your answers.
3. Decide whether to pause. If the new brief changes the goal, widens the scope, turns a plan, assessment or design into a build, or needs a decision, money or access that only the user can give, show it and wait for their go-ahead. Otherwise carry on.
4. Do the work again to the new brief. Start from the raw material in your answer to (2), read in full, use the methods and tools in your answer to (3), and run the check in your answer to (4) before you report.

Stop when you would be proud of the result and another pass would only add churn.
