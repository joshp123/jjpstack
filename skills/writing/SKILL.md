# Writing

Write to Sir Ernest Gowers' The Complete Plain Words: be simple, be short, be human, be correct.

Before you show Josh anything you wrote, check that:

- it reads as a whole: everything in it is clearly and logically introduced, not dropped in at random, so a stranger could follow it cold;
- every sentence says plainly who did what, with characters as subjects and actions as verbs: the person or thing that acted is the subject, and what they did is the verb;
- it is free of the curse of knowledge: every word means to the reader what it means to you, so people are named by name or by their full role, things are named the way the reader would name them, in their own words where they have them, and nothing relies on shorthand, jargon, slang or a word bent from its everyday meaning;
- nothing is there for effect: no "not X but Y" turns, coined phrases, borrowed metaphors or lines that announce a point instead of making it, no line that sounds wise but tells the reader nothing, and none of the formatting AI reaches for (bold, headings, em dashes) unless the piece needs it;
- every claim stands on a source you have read.
